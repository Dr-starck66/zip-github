Which advertised lease is cheapest versus which is actually cheaper?

Compare the Toyota Corolla LE and Hyundai Kona SE. Both carry a $239 advertised monthly payment in the selected October report, but the Kona asks for $1,000 more at signing. Spreading that difference over a 36-month lease changes the effective monthly comparison by roughly $28. Both examples require 10,000 miles per year and both were scheduled to end November 2, 2026. Neither $239 payment includes all local costs.

Then consider the Colorado: its $249 headline is close to the Corolla's, but its selected Trail Boss offer demands $4,999 upfront for only 24 months. The lease has a substantially larger monthly equivalent. That is a different deal than the less expensive-looking advertisement suggests.

How to compare car lease deals on equal terms

  1. Confirm exact model year, trim, drivetrain and advertised MSRP. A dealer's available vehicle may not match the advertisement.
  2. Record the complete cash due at signing, identify refundable deposits, acquisition fees and the first payment, and avoid counting any item twice.
  3. Use the same annual mileage assumption wherever possible. A 7,500-mile deal is not interchangeable with a 12,000-mile deal.
  4. Separate mandatory local taxes, registration and dealer charges from optional add-ons. Do not treat a quoted incentive as universally available.
  5. Compare a complete term's estimated outlay, then inspect end-of-term wear, disposition and early-termination terms.

What our comparison deliberately does not claim

These listings are editorial references, not binding dealership offers. We do not have direct dealer inventory, verified local quotes, credit approvals or an active referral relationship. Prices may depend on geography and credit eligibility, and some manufacturers require ownership of a competing vehicle or an existing lease. A complete written offer is the only reliable basis for deciding to sign.
