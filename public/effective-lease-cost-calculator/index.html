Why the first payment question matters

An advertisement may include the first monthly installment in the amount due at signing. If you count that installment again, you overstate total payment obligations. If it is genuinely due on top of the signing amount, subtracting one payment would understate the total. Ask your dealership for a line-item lease worksheet and use the appropriate setting.

Effective monthly cost is not your contractual monthly payment

The result called effective monthly cost is an arithmetic comparison of total scheduled cash outlay distributed evenly over the lease term. It does not rewrite the contract. You still owe the actual monthly installment, and the up-front amount comes due when the lease is executed. Comparing effective amounts is useful only if all offers are evaluated with the same taxes, payment counting, mileage assumptions and fees.

Try this verification example

A quote listing $239/month over 36 months and $2,999 at signing yields 35 additional installments if that cash contains the first payment. In that case, $239 × 35 + $2,999 equals $11,364, about $316 monthly equivalent. If the first installment is not in the signing cash, the result rises by $239 for the full term. This example is based on a reported October Corolla promotion, not a current dealer quote.

What the tool cannot measure without more data

Sales tax rules vary by state, and quotes may include unequal registration or acquisition fees. The residual value and purchase option affect whether buying the leased vehicle later makes sense. Disposition fees, excess miles, excess wear, early termination and the opportunity cost of tying up upfront cash can matter. Make a final decision only after reading the dealership's full written offer.
