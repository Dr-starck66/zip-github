Hidden car lease costs: what to ask before you sign

A lease advertised at one monthly number can carry additional expenses at signing, during use and when the car is returned. The contract—not a promotional price tag—defines those costs.

Acquisition and other startup charges

A lease may include an acquisition fee charged by the leasing company, a security deposit, registration, document-processing charges, local taxes and the first monthly payment. An acquisition fee is not automatically a dealer markup. Request a complete list of cash due at signing and ask the salesperson which entries are imposed by the lessor, the state or the dealer. If fees are capitalized into the lease, verify how doing so changes the monthly payment and total obligation.

Do not treat "zero down" as a synonym for "zero due at signing". A zero capitalized-cost reduction can still leave fees, tax or the first payment due.

Mileage, end-of-term wear and disposition

Most closed-end consumer leases specify an annual mileage allowance, a price for miles above it, return-condition standards and sometimes a disposition fee. Those are contingent liabilities: they are not necessarily included in an advertised monthly payment. Estimate actual driving from a recent odometer history, including commuting and travel; ask for an alternative quote with more annual miles if your expected use is higher.

Photograph vehicle condition at pickup and return, keep maintenance documentation, and request any inspection process available before return. Confirm what constitutes normal wear under the actual contract rather than relying on generic online dollar figures.

Insurance, repairs and early termination

A lease may require certain levels of physical-damage coverage and may include gap protection with exclusions. Review insurance requirements and who bears deductible or early termination obligations if the car is lost, stolen or damaged. Maintenance requirements and tire conditions may also be contractual.

An early termination quote can differ sharply from the remaining payments. Ask for the early termination method and a written example before signing, especially if you may relocate or change jobs.

Taxes and the effective monthly comparison

Local sales or use tax can be assessed differently by state and transaction structure. The appropriate comparison uses identical assumptions across quotes and separately identifies charges that were excluded from each advertisement. For simple screening, estimate (monthly payment × remaining installments + signing cash + known fees) / term; determine whether the first monthly installment is already part of signing cash to avoid double counting.

Consider a hypothetical 36-month lease with $250 per month and $3,000 due at signing, including the first payment. The scheduled outlay is 35 × $250 + $3,000 = $11,750, or roughly $326.39 per month equivalent, before omitted taxes and contingent end-of-term charges.

Pre-signing checklist

Request a detailed lease worksheet showing vehicle year, trim, MSRP, negotiated selling price, all rebates and eligibility, residual, money factor if applicable, number of payments, annual miles, any excess-mile rate, total signing cash and all recurring charges. Confirm every figure in the final contract. A written dealer quote is more valuable than an unsupported average from a comparison article.
